APPLICATION GROUP 01

Marine

From the propulsion engine to the ship’s electrical bus. Define the engine, power sources, operating stations and vessel requirements together.

  1. Engines, generators & shore power
  2. Engine control & power management
  3. Propulsion & onboard loads

AC / DC power managementCoordinate generators, storage, shore supply and the electrical load.

Typical system
A vessel with several generators, bus sections and shore connection; DC or hybrid designs also include converters and battery storage.
What ComAp controls
For AC systems: synchronisation, breaker sequencing, active/reactive load sharing and load-dependent start/stop. DC systems coordinate energy sources through conversion equipment. Emergency generation has its own defined operating arrangement.
Example product families
InteliGen 1000 Marine for source control; InteliMains 1010 Marine for shore, bus-tie and other breaker applications.
What to review with Araya
Single-line diagram, AC/DC architecture, reserve power, heavy consumers, shore transfer and protection requirements.

Engine controlSupervise propulsion, auxiliary, emergency and harbour engines.

Typical system
An engine with an electronic ECU or conventional sensors, local controls and remote operating displays.
What ComAp controls
Read speed and engine measurements, manage start/stop commands, display alarms and execute configured protection. Auxiliary equipment such as pre-lubrication pumps can be included in the agreed sequence.
Example product families
InteliDrive marine controllers, including InteliDrive 700 Marine; suitable I/O, displays and protection equipment.
What to review with Araya
Engine interfaces, signal list, local/remote authority, alarm and shutdown matrix, and operating stations.

Emissions reductionUse operating data and coordinated energy sources to review fuel use.

Typical system
A conventional or hybrid vessel with engine measurements, fuel data and, where fitted, batteries or alternative-fuel equipment.
What ComAp controls
Log operating data for comparison, coordinate hybrid power sources and support configured operating strategies. Bi-fuel operation uses dedicated fuel-control equipment alongside the controller.
Example product families
Marine PMS, engine control, data logging and specialist bi-fuel solutions selected for the installation.
What to review with Araya
Measured baseline, duty cycle, available signals, fuel system and the proposed operating strategy. Savings depend on the actual system.

Propulsion controlMatch mechanical or electric propulsion commands to the available machinery.

Typical system
A diesel-mechanical shaft line, or an electric propulsion motor driven by a variable-frequency drive on an AC or DC system.
What ComAp controls
Mechanical applications supervise the propulsion engine and its operating interfaces. Electric applications coordinate drive speed demand with available power reserve and the vessel’s power-management system.
Example product families
InteliDrive 700 Marine for engine supervision; InteliMains 1010 Marine for electric propulsion control, with suitable marine displays and PMS interfaces.
What to review with Araya
Propulsion layout, drive or gearbox interfaces, command stations, interlocks, feedback and required vessel documentation.

APPLICATION GROUP 02

Power generation

Start with the duty: standby during a mains failure, continuous site power, parallel generators or a specialist energy process.

  1. Mains & generating sets
  2. Start/stop, synchronisation & breakers
  3. Site loads & operating priorities

Standby & automatic mains failureStart a backup generator and transfer the load when mains power fails.

Typical system
One standby generator, mains sensing and interlocked source switching supplying a building or process.
What ComAp controls
Detect a mains failure, start and supervise the engine, transfer to generator supply, then return to mains and stop after the configured recovery sequence. Remote visibility needs the appropriate communications hardware.
Example product families
InteliLite AMF controllers for single-set automatic mains failure; WebSupervisor for a connected fleet.
What to review with Araya
Mains limits, transfer equipment, electrical/mechanical interlocks, start delays, return sequence and essential loads.

Prime powerOperate a generating set as the site’s main source of electricity.

Typical system
A single generating set serving a construction site, remote operation or other location without a dependable grid.
What ComAp controls
Manual or remote starting, engine and generator supervision, protection and operating history. A multiple-set installation also needs a paralleling architecture when the sets share one electrical bus.
Example product families
InteliLite manual/remote-start or suitable single-set controllers; InteliGen for a separate paralleling requirement.
What to review with Araya
Duty cycle, load steps, engine signals, site start demand and whether sets operate independently or in parallel.

Generator synchronisation & load sharingRun two or more sets together and share the electrical demand.

Typical system
Multiple generators supplying a common bus, with optional mains connection and bus-tie breakers.
What ComAp controls
Match the incoming generator before breaker closure, share active and reactive load, and start or stop sets around demand and configured reserve. Mains-parallel operation adds import/export and source-transfer requirements.
Example product families
InteliGen paralleling controllers, with suitable mains or tie-breaker control where required.
What to review with Araya
Governor/AVR interfaces, CT/VT data, breaker feedback, load reserve, operating modes and existing controller compatibility.

Hybrid power generationCombine generator sets with renewable generation and stored energy.

Typical system
Diesel generation, solar or other renewable sources and battery storage in a grid-connected or island installation.
What ComAp controls
Balance generation and load, coordinate source setpoints and battery use, retain operating reserve and manage renewable output around system limits. Grid-connected designs also define permitted import and export.
Example product families
InteliNeo microgrid controllers with suitable generator, inverter and storage interfaces.
What to review with Araya
Source ratings, inverter protocols, battery operating limits, grid/island modes and required reserve strategy.

Mission-critical powerDefine backup control around essential loads and failure scenarios.

Typical system
Critical facilities such as hospitals or datacentres with multiple power sources and coordinated switchgear.
What ComAp controls
Coordinate generator and breaker operation within an engineered backup scheme. The architecture must address source loss, maintenance states, communications failure and the loads that must remain supported.
Example product families
Application-specific generator, mains and bus-tie controllers; monitoring and redundancy chosen at system level.
What to review with Araya
Required availability, permitted interruption, load priorities, failure cases and an agreed commissioning/test sequence.

DatacentresCoordinate generating sets and switchgear with the facility power design.

Typical system
A datacentre with several generators, mains inputs and bus sections, coordinated with its wider backup infrastructure.
What ComAp controls
Supervise generators, synchronisation and source/bus breakers. Suitable architectures add controller and communications redundancy, common visualisation and site-specific operating logic.
Example product families
InteliGen 1000 and InteliMains 1010 are manufacturer examples for demanding generator and mains/tie-breaker control.
What to review with Araya
Electrical topology, redundancy targets, generator acceptance sequence, UPS interfaces and planned maintenance states. The controller is one part of the site’s overall redundancy design.

Combined heat & power (CHP)Coordinate electrical generation with useful heat recovery.

Typical system
A gas generating set with engine controls and a heat-recovery process, running alone or alongside the grid.
What ComAp controls
Manage generator operation, engine ECU interfaces or specified engine-control functions, and auxiliary process logic. Heat recovery and electrical demand need a coordinated operating sequence.
Example product families
InteliSys Gas solutions with appropriate engine interfaces, process I/O and displays.
What to review with Araya
Gas-engine model, ECU functions, fuel data, heat-demand sequence, process equipment and grid connection requirements.

Bi-fuel & hydrogenUse specialist controls for a defined alternative-fuel engine project.

Typical system
A suitable diesel engine with an engineered gas or hydrogen supply and injection arrangement.
What ComAp controls
Coordinate fuel substitution with engine speed, power and operating conditions using dedicated equipment. The project also requires an appropriate engine-protection and fuel-system design.
Example product families
InteliBifuel solutions and dedicated protection equipment, chosen for the engine and injection method.
What to review with Araya
Engine suitability, fuel composition, injection method, protection, approvals and conversion scope. This requires more than changing a generator controller.

Fuel cellsCoordinate the electrical output of fuel-cell generating systems.

Typical system
A fuel-cell power package connected to other generating modules or the grid through suitable conversion equipment.
What ComAp controls
Add electrical synchronisation, paralleling, load sharing and source-management functions where the package architecture supports them. Define how commands and measurements pass between the package and electrical controls.
Example product families
InteliGen-based electrical control is a manufacturer example; package-specific interfaces remain essential.
What to review with Araya
Package controller, inverter/converter arrangement, AC/DC interfaces, grid/island operation and load-sharing strategy.

Rental, telecom & light towersControl distributed power equipment and see its operating status.

Typical system
Rental generators, telecom-site power systems or light towers with local controls and, where required, a communications link.
What ComAp controls
Apply the start/stop and monitoring arrangement suited to each asset. Fleet supervision can bring connected equipment status and operating records into one view for service planning.
Example product families
Single-set or application-specific controllers, with WebSupervisor for supported connected devices.
What to review with Araya
Asset types, operating schedule, signals, network coverage, user access and required fleet reports.

Hybrid energy managementDecide how the site’s available energy sources work together.

Typical system

A battery energy storage system (BESS) with a battery management system (BMS), power conversion system (PCS) and auxiliary equipment.
What ComAp controls
Coordinate BMS and PCS data, charging/discharging commands, auxiliary monitoring and protection. Suitable arrangements add synchronising AC breaker control and integration with other microgrid assets.
Example product families
InteliNeo 530 BESS for storage-system control and interfaces to a higher-level energy manager.
What to review with Araya
BMS/PCS compatibility, battery limits, breaker arrangement, auxiliary I/O and on-grid/off-grid operating requirements.

Fleet managementReview connected equipment across several sites from one platform.

Typical system
Distributed controllers and communications gateways sending operating information to a cloud account.

Asset list, communications hardware, network access, subscriptions, alarm recipients, reports and user permissions.

A local monitoring PC connected to controllers, meters and supported third-party devices.
What ComAp controls
InteliSCADA brings measurements, alarm lists, historical trends and configured operating controls into a site-specific visualisation. Screen layout and available commands follow the connected equipment and access permissions.
Example product families
InteliSCADA with compatible controllers, communications and selected software functions.

Signal list, network topology, required screens, trend storage, operator roles and allowed commands.

COMAP GUIDE

Common questions

What is the difference between engine control and generator power management?

Engine control supervises the engine, including measurements, commands, alarms, and protection. Generator power management coordinates electrical sources and their breakers, including synchronisation and load sharing where required. Some projects need both functions and a defined interface between them.

Can I mix different ComAp controllers or replace an older model directly?

Compatibility is specific to the controller family, firmware, communication method, wiring, and operating sequence. A similar panel size does not establish compatibility. Share the existing models and configuration so a migration can be reviewed before hardware is selected.

Does every ComAp controller include load sharing or remote monitoring?

No. Features vary between controller families, software options, extension modules, and communication arrangements. InteliLite single-set control, InteliGen paralleling, and WebSupervisor cloud monitoring address different requirements. Confirm the complete configuration, licensing, and connectivity for your project.
